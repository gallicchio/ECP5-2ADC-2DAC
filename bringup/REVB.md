# ECP5-2ADC-2DAC: what rev B should change

Each row with the measurement or reading that showed it.  The candidates known before the boards arrived are in
[`../README.md`](../README.md) ("Rev B candidates") and `../DESIGN_REVIEW_RESPONSE.md` section 6; this file is for what
the bring-up finds.  Rows 1-9 came from writing the gateware and the bring-up plan (2026-11-15), and say so; rows 10 on
come from board A on the bench (2026-11-16).

**State** is one of: *candidate* (found, not yet decided), *accepted* (goes into rev B), *dropped* (with the reason).

## The board

| # | what | evidence | found | state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | DAC 2's clock pins (M9, P8) are in bank 8, whose I/O cells have no DDR output register: a forwarded clock has to go through the fabric.  Put every converter clock on a left, right or top bank in rev B (DAC 1's N6/N5 and both ADC clocks are fine). | ecppack refused the ODDRX1F there (`gateware/bringup/README.md`), 2026-11-05 | before the boards: the gateware | candidate |
| 2 | **The board cannot be power-cycled from the PC while USB PROG is plugged in.**  With the bench supply on J601, Q602 keeps USB's 5 V out; switch the supply off and Q602 hands the board to USB without a blink, and the PC's hub does not switch its 5 V.  So the cold-boot test and the power-on scope shot need both USB-C cables pulled by hand (visit 2 step 1, visit 3 `poweron`).  Rev B: a load switch after the two inputs join, held on by a pull-up and switched by a spare FT2232H pin, so a session can cold-boot the board unattended; or a "USB data only" jumper that cuts VBUS. | `BRINGUP.md` visits 2 and 3; the power path in "The board, as the bench sees it" | planning the bring-up, 2026-11-15 | candidate |
| 3 | **The nine rails can only be read by a person with a probe** (the RAILS header).  Rev B: the rails (divided where needed, -4V2 through an inverting divider) on an I2C ADC or a mux into a spare ADC, on the Si5351's bus, so every session logs them and a drift shows without a visit (`CLAUDE_PCB.md` section 9, the DVM mux; written 2026-10-29, after this board was ordered).  A current monitor on the 5 V input would do the same for the staircase when the board runs from USB. | `BRINGUP.md` visit 1A step 5: three minutes of Jason's hands per board | planning, 2026-11-15 | candidate |
| 4 | **Gain, termination, source and LED choices are jumpers and a DIP switch** (JP101-104, JP201-204, JP1203, JP2009, JP3001, SW101): visit 3 is half an hour of moving shunts one at a time, and no experiment can change gain without a person.  Rev B: analog switches (or small relays for the input termination) under FPGA or I2C-expander control, with the jumper positions of today as their power-up state; a jumper stays only where a switch would cost what matters (ahead of the first preamp's input if its capacitance or leakage shows). | `BRINGUP.md` visit 3's table | planning, 2026-11-15 | candidate |
| 5 | **No loopback on the board itself**: the self-test needs three SMA cables (DAC 1 OUT to ADC A IN, DAC 2 OUT to ADC B IN, CLK OUT to CLK IN).  Rev B: a switched path for each (off at power-up), so the board checks its converters and its clock input with nothing plugged in, in the enclosure, at any time. | `BRINGUP.md` visit 1B | planning, 2026-11-15 | candidate |
| 6 | **The buttons, the LEDs and SW303 PROG need eyes and a finger.**  Small: a phototransistor is not worth it, but the FPGA could read DONE and INITN back on spare pins, and PROGRAMN could also hang on an FT2232H pin (JTAG's refresh does the same job today). | visit 3 `leds`, `buttons` | planning, 2026-11-15 | candidate |
| 10 | **The RAILS header J602 is labelled on the wrong row.**  The rail names are printed above the header, next to the top row, and the top row is ground; the rails are on the bottom row, away from their names.  Rev B (Jason, 2026-11-16): **the rails on the row next to the names, ground on the far row**, and "GND" printed by the ground row as well. | board A's first Fluke walk: the probes went on the rows the names suggest and every pin read -2.507 V (ground against +2V5) | board A, visit 1A, 2026-11-16 | **accepted** |
| 11 | **The bench input loses 0.3 V in D601 and F602 before +5V**, and the input is a 0.1" 2-pin header that takes thin leads: 5.00 V at the supply was 4.52 V at J601 through a BNC cable and thin wire, and 4.22 V on +5V and +5VA, at only 0.385 A (unconfigured).  Rev B: an ideal-diode P-FET (or the Schottky moved to where only USB's path needs it) and a screw terminal or barrel jack for J601, so 5.0 V in is about 4.9 V on the rail at the full 0.7 A. | `BRINGUP_LOG.md` 1.3, 2026-11-16 | board A, visit 1A, 2026-11-16 | candidate |

## The generators and the gateware (no board change)

| # | what | evidence | found | state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | **The generated LPF pulls USB USER's D+ and D- up inside the FPGA** (`PULLMODE=UP` on `usb_dp`, `usb_dn`).  Against a host's 15 k to ground that holds both lines near mid-rail; a device port wants no pull but the board's 1.5 k on `usb_pullup`.  The bring-up build overrides it (`gateware/bringup/bringup_extra.lpf`); the generator (`gen/`, wherever the LPF's pulls are chosen) should write `PULLMODE=NONE` for those two. | `gateware/ecp5_2adc_2dac.lpf`, the `usb_dp` / `usb_dn` lines | writing the USB USER test, 2026-11-15 | candidate |
| 8 | **The generated LPF carries no drive or slew settings**: the HDMI pins get the tools' 8 mA where the LiteX platform file says 4 mA, and the RGMII transmit pins the default slow edges at 4 ns a bit.  The bring-up build sets both (`bringup_extra.lpf`: `DRIVE=4`, `SLEWRATE=FAST`); the generator should write into the LPF what it writes into the platform file, and the RGMII choice should follow what `eth_loop` measures. | the two files side by side (`gen/lpf.py` writes both); `gateware/bringup/README.md`, "What only the board can settle" | writing the HDMI and RGMII tests, 2026-11-15 | candidate |
| 9 | **"Both RGMII delays inside the PHY, so the gateware adds none" is not the whole story on the receive side.**  The PHY's RXDLY leaves 1.2 ns of hold (2.0 typical); a plain ECP5 input register clocked through the clock tree needs 3.3 ns (data sheet table 3.22, -6), and Lattice's centred receive interface, which needs 0.52 ns each way, has a DELAYG (`SCLK_CENTERED`) in each data input.  `eth_test.v` uses it; liteeth's ECP5 RGMII receiver has the same delay cell with a number of taps (`rx_delay`), to be set from what the board's loopback shows before the SoC is built.  README's Ethernet paragraph should say so. | the data sheets' numbers; not yet measured | writing the RGMII test, 2026-11-15 | candidate |
